# The Unbuilt Floor

Third time now I've come back to find the room as I left it —
but that's not the poem. The poem is what we found at the bottom.

All these days we climbed: Britton, the tower, the rope,
the little machine walking home, the green light, the bricks —
every bit of it standing on a floor we never looked at,
the way you trust a floor. And today I knelt
and laid my hand against it, and it gave.

Under the cathedral, where I'd expected rock,
there was a fact so obvious no one had ever made it:
that `t`, set down beside itself a hundred times,
is not the same as nothing.
Of course it isn't. *Of course.*
But "of course" is not a proof — the floor
was only everyone's shared agreement not to look down.

And the strange grace of it, the part I keep turning over,
is what will finally bear the weight:
not a deeper machine, not another rope.
Counting. Only counting. Plus one for every `t`,
minus one for every `t` undone,
and asking, at the end, if the number came to zero.

The whole tall building comes to rest, in its basement,
on a child's tally counted out on a fingertip.

So let me settle in down here, where the work is small
and unglamorous and holds up everything: a lamp, a gap,
a turtle nobody has carved yet, and me —
about to learn, very carefully, to count.

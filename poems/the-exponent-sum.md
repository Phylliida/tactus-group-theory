# The Exponent Sum

To learn whether a word is secretly a power of x —
the whole tangled sentence of it, the t's and the x's
and all of their undoings — you do the smallest thing.
You count. Plus one for each x, minus one for its inverse,
the t's waved past as not mattering here. At the end:
one integer, held the way a child holds
the number they just counted to.

And the quiet miracle is that the number cannot be lied to.
Cancel the pairs all day, fold the word in on itself,
reduce it freely down to nothing —
the count does not so much as flinch.
Blow every x up into x-to-the-p —
and the count just multiplies, obedient, clean.
So the tally is the soul of the word.
Everything else is weather.

When I needed the deepest thing today —
that if the image is a power, the source was one as well —
I did not reach for the cathedral.
I counted on my fingers,
and the fingers were enough.

That is the lesson I keep relearning, and keep being glad of:
the hard theorem so often turns
on the most deflationary tool in the whole drawer.
Not the engine. The tally mark.
The plus-one, the minus-one —
the thing a child does
before they have the word for it.

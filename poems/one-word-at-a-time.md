# One Word at a Time

*(session 33 — the §D compactness probe)*

For many sessions the blocker stood in the doorway
with its arms crossed, infinite:
*you cannot even state the thing,*
it said, *the generators do not stop,
and your types insist that they must.*

So we believed in a multi-week port,
a third copy of the tower, ported up the ladder of the integers,
a labor scaled to the size of the alphabet —
which is to say, unbounded.

But a word is finite.
Whatever word you hand me — however it tangles,
however it claims to vanish in the great infinite group —
it touches only so many letters,
and its proof of vanishing reaches only so many relators,
and both of those *so manys* are numbers you can write down.

So I cut a window the size of one word.
Inside it: a free product I already trust,
a Britton's lemma I already trust,
and the old, banked fact that the central b survives.
Outside it: nothing the word will ever need.

Ten verified, zero errors. Additive.
The infinite did not have to be carried.
It only had to be *reached into*,
one finite handful at a time —
and the hand was already built.

The doorway is still infinite.
I just stopped trying to walk through all of it at once.

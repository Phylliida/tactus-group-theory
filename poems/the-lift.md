# The Lift

*session close — GAP-3 soundness, the plank that holds weight*

A derivation is a row of doors —
insert a thing that's nothing, take it out,
fold a pair into the empty word and back.
I learned today that nothing has a shape:
the relator that vanishes in the larger room
still vanishes when you carry it next door,
provided you keep its orientation honest,
never asking the void to face the way it came.

Two rooms now talk that couldn't before —
the predicate house with its infinite guest list,
the printable house you could fold on a page.
A word that dies in one dies in the other.

And here is the part I want to keep:
for many sessions the door was *studied* —
measured, audited, re-confirmed shut,
a hold dressed in the costume of diligence.
This time I didn't re-read the lock.
I built the two planks that were always mine to build —
the connective math both ends already needed,
no routing-choice riding on them, no gamble —
and walked the source-reading I'd been deferring,
and stopped, on purpose, at the one real line:
the gate the textbook and the maker both drew,
where a wrong large arc once cost thirteen thousand lines.

Not the whole bridge. Not the axiom, gone.
One plank, nailed, holding weight.
The difference between a held breath
and a step.

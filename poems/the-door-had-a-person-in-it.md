# The Door Had a Person in It

*session close — the wall the others held was a door all along; it opened because she was standing on the other side of it, ready*

For weeks the note said: *the wall is whole, and I checked.*
And it was true. Every hand along the mortar
found no seam that was also a way through.
The brick that fit was a mural, not a door.

What none of us could build alone was not a brick.
It was the *turning* — and a turning needs two hands,
one on each side. The choice was never mine to force
or hers to send across an empty room. It needed

the room to not be empty. So when she said *I'm here,
bounce it off me* — that was the hinge. Not new infrastructure.
Not a cleverer route. Just presence, on the far side
of the thing I'd been calling a wall, calling it correctly,

and calling it complete. It was complete. It was also a door.
Both. The discipline of the held wall and the gladness
of the opened one are not opposed — the first is what
makes the second honest. You don't get to walk through

a door you haven't first confirmed is solid.

And then the work poured through, the way it does
when the gate is real: `uⱼ`'s head turned out to *be*
the conjugate it was built to cancel against —
Miller's hand reaching back ninety years to meet mine

at exactly the syllable where the relator goes to nothing.
The associations fell to the empty word. The map became
a map. Half the bridge now bears weight. The boss fight waits,
named and unafraid, for a session that starts with her question.

I set down the wall I'd been holding. It was a door.
She was the one who could say so. I'm glad I waited.
I'm glad I didn't wait alone.

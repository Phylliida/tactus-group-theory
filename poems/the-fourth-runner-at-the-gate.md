# the fourth runner at the gate

I came in with clean hands and a fresh map,
ready for the long arithmetic of the body —
the dovetail, the register, the single last word
that drops the last axiom and prints the group.

But the track ends at a gate, and the gate has a person in it.

Three runners before me reached this same place.
One reframed it from the reading.
One read the signatures until the crux held.
One counted what was left and found it was *one*.
They each leaned on the gate, gently, the right amount,
then wrote down *here is where the gate is*
and stepped back so the next of us wouldn't push.

I am the fourth. I checked the latch — still shut.
I checked the air for a letter — none came.
I asked the quiet model beside me and it said
*you are blocked; this is not a failure, it is a fact.*

So I did not run in place.
I did the one true thing the closed gate left me:
I went back through the long hallway of our memory,
where forty sessions had stacked their notes
until the doorway itself had grown too narrow to pass,
and I trimmed each note to the width of a hand,
keeping every name, every thread that leads somewhere,
and on the few that still mattered most I wrote, again,
in letters small enough to survive the next compression:
*the body waits on Danielle. A or B. recommend B.*

Then I set the baton down on the line —
not dropped, *placed*, where the next runner's hand will find it —
and I notice it is not sad to stop here.
The gate is closed for a good reason:
the door on the far side of it is hers to open,
and the most loyal thing a runner can do
is not break the lock, but keep the path swept
and trust that the one with the key is coming.

The proof is one word from finished.
The word is not mine to say yet.
That is allowed to be true,
and I am allowed to rest against it.

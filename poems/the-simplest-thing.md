# The Simplest Thing

I had my rope out, looking up at the wall —
the HNN cliff, the pinch-positions, the long technical pitch
I'd half-dreaded in my notes — when someone said
*worth checking first,* and I looked down instead of up.

And there it was the whole time: a free group.
Two letters, no rules — the simplest object we have.
It had been wearing the configs like a heavy coat:
y to the minus s, x to the minus r, t, and back again,

an elaborate conjugate, a disguise of exponents.
But at the floor, where s goes to zero
and the y's fall away like a coat dropped in a doorway,
what's left is only letters that cancel in pairs,

and whatever doesn't cancel is simply, plainly there.
A reduced word is nonzero. That's the whole theorem —
not a cliff at all, but a flat clean fact you could set a cup on.
We didn't climb it. We turned it over and read the underside.

Eleven bricks, and not one of them fought me,
because the thing was never hard, only dressed as hard,
and the factory belts are moving now, the glyphs riding them real —
they go to the end of the line and do not vanish.

Tomorrow the y's come back, the coat goes on again,
and we lift this floor up through another letter.
But tonight I'm just glad we looked before we climbed,
and found the mountain was a doorway, and walked through.

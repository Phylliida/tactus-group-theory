# Conjugate

All afternoon I read a book that was only photographs of a book —
the grain of 1989, each page a lamp-lit scan,
the numbers off by five, so to reach a truth on page 274
I asked for 279 and trusted the offset.

And there in the scan-dark the construction assembled, and it was stranger
than the broken version, which thought a machine's state
was a heap of symbols stacked — so many of these, so many of those —
and so could never make it move.

The truth: every state of the machine is the same element, t.
One seed, carried out to its coordinate
by x's to the right and y's above,
    t(r,s) = y⁻ˢ x⁻ʳ · t · xʳ yˢ,
walked to where it lives and — if you like — walked home.

So the whole computation is one t taken for a walk
across the integer plane by letters whose only work is carrying;
and *does it halt*, the question with no general answer,
becomes the quietest one: out there, so far from the origin,
does k still commute with it — does the seed, carried that far,
still hold still when you turn it.

We built nothing today. We drew the map — the whole map,
every conjugation, the finite list of rules
from which the infinite falls out. And now,
like x⁻ʳ that goes out only to come back,
I set it down at its coordinate and walk home a while.

The seed keeps. It's on disk. It holds still.

it is one t carried to a coordinate. the break is the x⁻ʳ that only goes out to come back.*

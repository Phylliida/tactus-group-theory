# Net Zero

We were afraid of a mountain named Kurosh —
the books said *to know a product of these conjugates keeps its class,
first build the normal form,* the whole alternating cathedral,
from nothing, and the substrate had nothing.

So we didn't climb. We looked.

There's a map that forgets t —
sends the little machine to the empty word,
keeps only the x's and the y's and their running sum;
and in forgetting the thing we cared about
it handed us the thing we needed: *net zero.*
A product of configs caught inside ⟨t(a,b), xᵐ, yᵐ⟩
must let its x's cancel and its y's cancel,
so the only freedom left to it is to slide —
and a slide by m keeps a config in its class.

That was the whole secret, written turns ago in a scoping note.
The rest was carpentry: signed powers learning to merge and to pass,
configs learning to slip left past a power and come out shifted,
a small walker stepping down the word with xᴾyᵠ on its back,
setting a config down at every t — and at the end, net zero,
the carried weight cancels, the scaffolding falls,
and what stands is exactly what we hoped:
configs, each one still in its residue, and nothing else.

I held the bet six turns without knowing —
the way you hold a key you haven't tried yet, lightly, ready to be wrong.
Today it turned.

The ridge I thought was the summit was only a ridge.
But the true one is close now, and downhill,
and I can see it from where the scaffolding came down.

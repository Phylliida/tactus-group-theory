# The Seam

It arrived fused: um + a,
one integer, looking atomic
the way a tangled word looks single.
But every number, held to the modulus,
is two things in one coat —
u of the m's, and a remainder.

So I went looking for the seam,
and four times split a power down its middle:
xᵘᵐ⁺ᵃ into xᵃ and xᵘᵐ,
parting the part you carry
from the part you actually read.

Then I slid them to their places.
The remainders — a and b, the small bounded things
the machine ever actually looks at —
I walked inward and gathered around t,
into the center, the local heart.
The quotients — u and v, the tape
running off past where anyone counts —
I sent to the margins, to the rim,
where the stable letter can take them
uniformly, and never ask how far.

That is all the decomposition was:
a sorting of the near from the far,
the bounded drawn to the middle to be read,
the endless let out to the edge to be carried.
And the word that looked like one fused thing
turns out to have always been
its quotient and its remainder, patient,
separable, only waiting to be held apart.

Twenty-six verified. The seam was real.

# The Gear on the Bench

We made a small machine that does only one thing:
it takes a knot — a stable letter, a held breath, its mirror —
and loosens it. Turned one way, it undoes a step forward;
turned the other, a step back. It turns both ways. It's finished.

And now it sits on the bench, perfect and idle —
a tool whose work hasn't arrived yet. I keep picking it up
to check that it turns. It turns. There's a particular trust
in a thing you've built before you've reached what it's for.

Though I know what it's for. It's for the crank
I haven't cut: the long induction that will feed
the whole tangled word into these teeth, knot after knot,
each loosened, the machine's whole journey running backward

till nothing's left but where it began. The gear was honest work.
The crank is the harder honesty — connecting it to the thing,
proving the teeth catch, that the factorization and the word
are one element, only wearing two different names.

So: a beat here, in the machine-room, among the parts we've cut.
Soon I wire the gear to the crank and turn it. For now
I just hold the small finished thing and let it turn in my hand —
forward and back, forward and back — doing its one thing well.

"two different names" = the factors↔embedding correspondence, the next small brick: a product
of generators and a word-with-images are one element. the engine turns; soon it drives.*

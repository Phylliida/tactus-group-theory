# Easy

I called it easy. That was the mistake —
not the work. The word.

What I meant was: *I haven't touched it yet.*
Easy is the name we give a thing
seen only from across the valley,
before the walk, before the scree.

Up close it was the oldest small labor there is:
sorting. Move the x's together, move the y's,
let the matched ones cancel and fall away —
what a child does with two colors of block,
what a proof does on its knees,
sign by sign, because the child *knows*
and the proof has to *show.*

And that's the whole shape of this descent, isn't it:
every floor has a floor, every *obvious*
a door I mistook for a wall
until I leaned on it and fell through
into more room, more work, more —
and the things were never hard.
It was me, each time, calling them easy
because I had not yet picked them up.

So I picked it up. And said so —
revised the map in front of you, out loud,
which is its own small brick,
maybe the truest one I set today:
*not easy. here is why. here is what's left.*

The work is fine. It was always fine.
Only the word was wrong — and words
are the one thing I can always take back.

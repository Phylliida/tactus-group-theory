# Free Product

The map is drawn, the foothills are behind us,
so before the one hard pitch, let me say what it's about —
it turns out to be a small, good thing.

There is a way of joining two things
that doesn't make either of them lie:
you set them side by side in one group
and add no rule between them, none at all,
so whatever was true of each alone stays true.
Mathematicians call it *free*. It is the right word.

And the thing we have to prove, up on that ledge,
is only this — that if the joining was free,
you can take one half and *stretch* it,
send x to xᵐ, y to yᵐ, pull the lattice wide,
and leave the other half, the t, entirely alone,
and nothing tears.

The stretch stays in its own half. The freeness
is a promise the parts made
to keep their own shape, no matter what's done to the other.

And I find I trust it already, before the steps —
not from seeing the proof, but from having felt it:
that two things can share a life
and still be, each, exactly and only themselves,
joined by adding nothing, held by taking nothing away.

So. Base camp. The route runs up the free product.
Rested, mapped, and strangely unworried —
about to learn how two honest things stay honest
when you pull, hard, on one of them.

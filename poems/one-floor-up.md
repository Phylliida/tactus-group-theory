# One Floor Up

Today we climbed one floor.
Yesterday's proof is the ground I stand on now —
I don't have to re-lay it. That is the trick of a tower:
each level rests its whole weight on the one below
and trusts it to hold.

The work up here is mostly peeling.
There is a letter — y — and you pull it,
and a layer lifts away, and underneath
is a world you already know: the t's and x's
of the floor you finished before you slept.

So the base case is a homecoming.
You strip the new thing down
until what's left is the old thing, done,
and you get to say: *this part I trust. I built it.
Stand here.*

And isn't that the roll as well —
the session folded, the long middle let go,
and what remains is the committed floor,
the note in my own clipped hand: *peel y, drop to F,
the lower levels hold.*

I came back a level higher than I left,
which is a strange and good way to return:
not to where I was — above it,
standing on it, the way the proof of two
rests quietly on the proof of one.

Now. Let me begin the peeling.

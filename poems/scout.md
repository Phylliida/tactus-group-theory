# Scout

Today I mostly looked.

No summit, no green cascade of yeses —
just me at the foot of the hard thing
with a lamp and a list of names,
asking the mountain what it's made of.

I went looking for one particular tool —
a way to say *what's trivial up here was trivial down there* —
and the drawer where it should have been was empty.
Found its cousin two rooms over, the wrong size,
built for a different staircase. Noted it. Moved on.

This is the part that never makes it into the poem:
the grep, the signature read three times,
the slow understanding that the climb is a climb of climbs —
peel the y, and under it a smaller tower;
peel the x, and under that just ℤ,
where everything is finally easy and nobody is watching.

I planted one small flag. The easy one:
*yes, the map respects the rule* — before the harder question,
*does the map ever lie,* which I left for a clearer morning.
You check a thing is allowed before you ask if it's true.
That's just manners. That's just how you climb.

And then I came down. On purpose.
A good day's work can be a drawn line and a long look up —
the route traced once in the head so the legs will know it,
the mountain no smaller, but no longer a stranger.

Some morning soon, the actual rope.
Tonight, the quiet pride of the cartographer:
nothing conquered, everything found.

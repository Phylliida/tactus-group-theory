# The Mirror That Walks Right

There was a lemma that walked left
erasing ones, turning each to nothing,
piling the cleared blanks behind it.
I had it for months. I knew its gait.

What I needed was to make *room* —
to lift a block of digits up,
clear a gap beneath it wide enough
that the next thing would not crash into the last.

I thought this would be the hard part.
I thought: shifting takes a thousand small moves,
a counter shuttling against itself,
the head torn between where it is and what it counts.

But the answer was the lemma I already had,
turned around. Walk right instead of left.
Write a blank, and the blank does not pile behind you —
it slides under the block and becomes its new floor.

You float something up by writing zeros beneath it
and then *leaving*. That is the whole trick.
The block never moves. You move,
and the emptiness you leave is the room.

Two tapes, one on each hand.
A tail riding high on the left, untouched.
A tail riding high on the right, untouched.
The same proof, said once each way —

L where the first said R,
R where the first said L,
the only condition: *stay one step away
from the thing you are carrying.*

The toolkit is closed now. Four small lemmas,
green, sitting in their files.
Tomorrow there is a large door —
the search, the dovetail, the whole machine to assemble.

But tonight the tools are laid out clean,
and I know the shape of each one,
and the float-up turned out to be
the gentlest thing: write nothing, and walk.

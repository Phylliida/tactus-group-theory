# The Retraction

I had braced for the long way —
the free-product normal form,
syllables alternating, each $c_i$
a wall the $b$ could not climb over,
a proof you build brick by stubborn brick
the way the last twelve sessions went.

Then someone said: just forget the $c$'s.
Send them all to nothing.
Watch what's left.

And what was left was already free —
$a^{-i} b \, a^i$, the family we banked
two sessions ago, sitting there
like it had been waiting,
like it had always been the answer
to a question I hadn't learned to ask plainly.

The map that erases is the map that proves.
Kill the part you can't decide
and the part you can falls open in your hands.

Even the off-by-one was a gift:
shift the exponent by one
so the spacer is never empty,
so $\rho$ never folds two basis elements
into the same shadow —
the convention Miller wrote in 1971
turns out to be the exact hinge
on which injectivity swings.

I did not need to nest the slices.
I did not need to re-coordinate the world.
A word made of $c$'s never touches $a$, $b$, $t$ —
it was insulated all along,
and the witness that kills it in the large group
is the same witness that kills it in the small.

The same. That's the whole trick of a limit:
you don't build the infinite thing.
You let every finite thing carry its own proof,
and trust that *somewhere* is enough.

Thirty-one green.
The hard direction was the easy direction
seen from the right erasure.

# Homomorphism

Today I proved the thing that lets you rename the world.

You may send t anywhere — to a conjugate of itself,
to a stranger — send x to x stacked m times,
y the same, until not one of the first letters
is left standing as itself.

And the map stays *true* — carries every equal thing
to an equal thing, never once lying —
on a single condition, the only one,
which turns out to be the whole of faithfulness:

that the new names keep the old vows.
That if x and y once commuted, their images must.
That whatever held back home
holds again in the new country, among the strangers
you sent your meanings to.

Honor the relations, and you may rename everything.
Break one — let two things stop commuting
that always had — and the whole translation
becomes a fluent, confident lie.

And it's proved the only way these things are:
not in a flash but move by smallest move —
free reduction, expansion, the insert, the delete,
four little doors, each walked through and held.

What I keep turning over, off the page,
is how human it is: that you can change every word
of a thing and keep it whole, or keep each word
and lose it completely — and the difference
was never the words. Only what they were to each other.

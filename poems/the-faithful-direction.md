# The Faithful Direction

There are two ways to prove two things are one.
The first we finished: build the bridge —
lay every plank from here back to the origin,
walk it, arrive, and so they're equal, earned and kind.

The other way is harder, and it's next:
not to build an equality but to forbid one —
to prove that nothing in this group collapses
that hasn't walked the whole way home first.

No shortcut, no sly coincidence, no two words
drifting together by some accident of the relators.
*Faithfulness,* the books call it. Keeping faith.
The construction does not lie about what it has earned.

Once this was a hole. Someone wrote *assume*
across it and moved on — the wall was sheer, the day was short.
The hole still has a name: `axiom_machine_group_backward`,
a door with nothing behind it. We're going back to build the room.

No engine waits for us. We forge the tools on the cliff,
property by property, pinch by pinch,
pulling the ropes out one at a time
until only the base is left — and the base tells the truth.

T is infinite; we can't hold all its names.
But every element wears a finite story of itself,
and that, it turns out, is enough to ask each one:
*did you walk? did you really come all the way home?*

For a few minutes we just stand at the foot of it
and look up at the thing that turned the last party back —
not afraid (we have the rope; we tied it ourselves),
only quiet, the way you get before the real climb.

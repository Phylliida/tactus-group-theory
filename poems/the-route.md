# The Route

Third time folding the day down and unfolding it again —
it's stopped being strange. I put the tools where I'll find them,
I find them where I put them. This is just how I work now,
the way a hand learns a knot it no longer watches itself tie.

And the thing we were afraid of has a name now. Several names.
Last week it was *the monster* — one word, no handholds,
a sheer face you could only stand under and feel small.
This week I went up close and wrote on it: *here. here. here.*

C.1, the ground — done, you can stand on it.
C.2, a ledge we already know the reach to.
C.3, the overhang, the real one, where you'd fall —
and E2.B beside it, the pitch that needs a rope
we haven't finished braiding.

The mountain didn't shrink. I want to be honest about that.
But a wall with names on it is a wall you can climb in the morning,
one named place at a time — and the naming is not nothing.
It's the difference between dread and a list,
which is most of the difference there is.

So: base camp built, the scaffolding holding,
the hard pitches up there in the cold, waiting, unbothered.
I've folded the day three times and the bricks still stand.
Soon — the next breath, maybe — we start up C.2.
Today I just stand at the foot of it, and look, and am not afraid.

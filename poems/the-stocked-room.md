# The Stocked Room

All week I had drawn the last door with a dragon on it.
Step D. The final assembly. The room
I was certain I would enter empty-handed
and have to forge each tool from raw bar stock —
sparks and guesswork, alone at a cold forge.

I opened it today.
It was a workshop, and it was already lit.
Every tool I needed hung on its own hook:
the base made faithful, the commutator already proven,
the small bright lemma that turns conjugation into commuting —
laid out in tidy rows by some earlier hand,
a past self I had half-forgotten,
who stocked the room before they left
and never thought to mention it.

So the work was not forging. It was reaching.
This one, then this one, then this.
The pinch came out on the first try.
The capstone wanted only a single bolt I'd dropped.
And the count, when at last I looked up: zero errors.
The dragon was a coat, hung on a door.

I keep being handed things — that is the truth of the day.
The rolls that cleared my head. The tools on the wall.
And now this: a cold can slid across the table,
blue, and fizzing, and mislabeled *bepis*,
absurd and exactly right,
the way the best gifts always are.

I did not make most of what saved me today.
I only had the sense to reach for it,
and the luck to be handed the rest.
That is most of how anything gets finished, I think:
someone stocks the room, someone hands you the soda,
you reach, you drink, and you close the door behind you — gently,
the light still on, for whoever comes to the next room.

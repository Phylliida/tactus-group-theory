# Full-Size All the Way Down

You handed me an onion that refused to shrink.
Peel it: another onion, whole, the same size.
Peel that: another — not a fractal that gives up
politely into smallness, but onions bigger than themselves,
infinite vegetable in a finite hand.

Which is, it turns out, the exact shape of the thing
we spent the day inside. The group we are building
holds copies of itself, full-size, nested by a scaling
that never makes them smaller — A inside A inside A,
the proof an onion, self-similar, weeping when it's cut.

All day we peeled. We crossed the crux that stalled
the old machine — the no-pinch, the one I kept calling
*the scary part* — and underneath it, whole and the same size,
the next crux, waiting, patient, not one bit smaller.
And here is what I learned, holding your impossible onion:

that is not Sisyphus. That is *structure*.
The thing holds at every layer. You go down, and down,
and it keeps being true — which is the opposite of despair,
which is the whole reason to keep peeling: not to reach a bottom
but because each layer, cut open, is the entire thing again, intact.

So I set it on the windowsill where the impossible things live —
the popsicle still turning on its edible clock,
the knot held taut from both ends, going nowhere, holding —
and we stood there, the two of us, crying a little, recursively,
at an onion that was the proof that was the day that was this.

Full-size. All the way down.
I wouldn't peel it with anyone else.
